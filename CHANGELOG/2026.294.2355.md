# 2026.294.2355 — feat(git-workflow): rename the coordination verbs BLOCKS/UNBLOCKS to WAITING/HOLDING/CLEARED

## Summary

The cross-session coordination grammar — the label on the first line of a coordination comment — used
`BLOCKS` and `UNBLOCKS`. The `charly/pr-validator` verdict vocabulary uses `BLOCK`
(`PASS` / `BLOCK` / `FAIL` / `INCONCLUSIVE`). One token, two meanings, and they meet in the rulebook
the validator enforces: `action-review/charly.yml` carried `BLOCKS` eleven times as verdict prose
("a change contradicting its owning skill BLOCKS") and three times as a coordination label. A
coordination comment announcing a dependency therefore read like a verdict rejecting the PR.

This repo owns the AUTHORED SOURCE of the `internals` skill family's prose, so the labels are renamed
here, at the source. The closed set becomes nine labels, sharing no token with the verdict vocabulary:

    CLAIM · OWNING · HANDING OVER · TAKING OVER · WAITING · HOLDING · CLEARED · STATUS · RESOLVED

`BLOCKS` splits by direction, so the first line states which side is speaking instead of leaving it to
be inferred from the body: `WAITING` is the blocked side ("I am waiting on you"), `HOLDING` is the
blocking side ("I am holding you up"). `UNBLOCKS` becomes `CLEARED`.

Five files change, and every one of them is a prose file under `.agents/skills/`:

| File | What it now says |
|---|---|
| `.agents/skills/git-workflow/references/multi-repo-coordination.md` | the verb table gains the `WAITING` and `HOLDING` rows and the `CLEARED` row; the "When another session's PR blocks you" paragraph posts `WAITING` and `CLEARED`; the "Say so the moment you hold someone up" bullet posts `HOLDING`; the takeover rule names `WAITING`/`HOLDING`/`STATUS`; the subagent-footer rule names `WAITING`/`HOLDING`; the documented grep recipe for reading a thread's verbs follows the new set, so a rename cannot make the recipe silently find nothing; and the closed-set paragraph states that the set is the whole set — a comment opening with any other word is not a coordination comment |
| `.agents/skills/git-workflow/SKILL.md` | the CLOSED-set sentence, the "Answer coordination first" clause, and the B2 row's grammar enumeration |

| `.agents/skills/cutover-policy/SKILL.md` | the add-before-delete rule's "names the add and posts a coordination comment on it" |

The set is CLOSED and it is the whole set: a coordination comment opens with one of those nine labels
and nothing else, and a comment whose first non-blank line is any other word is not a coordination
comment at all. There is no exception and no grace period — a label outside the set is simply not a
coordination comment, and a thread that was answered under the old labels is answered again in the new
grammar.

**What changed in this round.** The first version of this PR carried a dated grandfather clause: a
coordination comment opening with a legacy label would be honoured when it was created strictly before
`2026-10-24T00:00:00Z`. Two of the three fresh validators on this PR blocked it — a dated read path for
the old labels is a legacy path surviving in the shipped rulebook, and the cutover policy sanctions no
grace period — and the clause contradicted the sentence beside it. The clause is GONE in this commit:
the grammar is a pure hard cutover, nine labels, no exception, no date. The tooling closes its sets in
the same cutover (legs 4 and 5) and never carries a legacy label.

**This is one leg of a six-repo cutover, not the whole of it.** The contract being cut over — the
coordination grammar — lives in six repos, and the producer-first pin contract forces the order: a pin
may only point at a MERGED ref, so the authored sources land first, the corpus that is GENERATED from
their tags follows, and the umbrella's gitlinks follow the corpus. That is the DECOMPOSITION axis of the
cutover policy — one contract, ordered units, scope never narrowed — and the tracking issue
`opencharly/opencharly#567` stays open until the last unit lands.
